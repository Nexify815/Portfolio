The problem

Small kitchens run on notebooks and memory. Sales, stock, staff rotas and supplier orders live in different places, so the state of the business is never visible at a glance and stockouts are discovered too late.

What I built

A full-stack restaurant management platform that keeps the operational side of a kitchen in one system.

01

Point of sale

Menu grid, size selection, extras and cash, mobile-money or card payments.

02

Sales tracking

Daily and weekly dashboards, revenue charts, category breakdown and payment-method stats.

03

Inventory

Low-stock alerts, automatic deduction on sale and adjustment logging.

04

Staff management

Shift scheduling, task assignment, time tracking and closing cash reconciliation.

05

Expenses

Categorised by payment method with budget alerts.

06

CRM and purchasing

Customers and suppliers, including purchase orders.

07

Backup and restore

The full database as JSON.

Access and accountability

Money and stock move through several pairs of hands, so access is role-based (owner and staff) and every create, update and delete is logged with who did it and when. The API uses JWT in httpOnly cookies with bcrypt hashing, plus Helmet, rate limiting, CORS and CSRF double-submit protection.

Decisions that mattered

PostgreSQL in production, SQLite locally

Behind one database adapter, for fast local development without maintaining a second schema.

An installable PWA

The kitchen can put it on a phone or tablet without an app store.

Full-database JSON export

So a small business can take its own data with it.

Outcome

The production system runs Chef Jhamin's Kitchen. The public demo is a sanitized version with synthetic data that exercises every feature.
