Nexisploit: All-in-One Android ADB Toolkit

An open toolkit I built for doing anything with an Android phone from a laptop. One menu, 165 tools, and built-in checks that tell you how safe your phone really is. It runs on Windows, Linux and macOS.

Role
Design and Python development
Stack
Python, ADB and scrcpy, with Nmap and Metasploit underneath
Platform
Windows, Linux and macOS command line

Built on top of PhoneSploit Pro by Azeem Idrisi, released under the GNU GPL v3. Maintained as github.com/Nexify815/nexisploit.

The problem

Controlling an Android phone from a laptop usually means a dozen half-remembered commands, and every risky one sitting a careless keypress away from wiping data. The tool that already existed tried to do everything, and ended up hard to find anything in. I wanted one place with one plain menu, where common things are obvious and dangerous things can't happen by accident.

What I built

An open-source toolkit rebuilt from its original shape into something you can actually find your way around. Nexisploit talks to a phone over USB or Wi-Fi and puts everything it can do in one numbered menu: mirror the screen, install apps, pull photos and messages, check how safe the phone is, and watch it work in real time.

01

A menu you can't get lost in

Fifteen plain-language groups, your six most recent tools pinned to the top, type a few letters to search, and always-visible phone status in the corner.

02

Is your phone safe?

Fourteen free checks: patch date, encryption, app permissions, debuggable apps and more, each answered with OK, review this or fix this.

03

Cameras that obey

List every camera, view it live, record it, or watch for motion. Sizes and frame rates are read from the phone itself, never guessed.

04

It watches in real time

CPU, memory, battery, Wi-Fi, a route-tracker that maps where the phone went, and a wall of every waiting notification.

05

A heads-up on the screen

Draws boxes around what the phone is showing, marks where it was tapped, and shows its inner workings live, right on the mirrored screen.

06

Reports that matter

Battery health, screen time, what is eating storage, colour-coded logs. Cleanups show a preview first and nothing deletes until you confirm.

How it is built

Language
Python
One terminal program, no framework, no accounts
Phone bridge
ADB and scrcpy
The phone's built-in debugging bridge, plus a screen mirror
Network
Nmap
Finds phones on the Wi-Fi and recognises them
Attack tool
Metasploit
For the one attack flow, kept separate and deliberate

Runs on any laptop; the phone only needs developer options and USB debugging, which every Android has.

Decisions that mattered

A menu, not a puzzle

The old one made you remember numbers. Now you pick a group with plain words first, then a number, with a live phone status in the corner so you know it is connected.

Group by the job, not the thing

All the file tools stay together, even when the file is a photo. Everything that runs on this laptop gets its own group, not a junk drawer.

Checks built in, not bolted on

The original depended on a separate audit program nobody else had installed. Every check now runs from the tool itself, anywhere the phone answers.

Harmless by accident

Anything that wipes or uninstalls asks you to type YES. Anything that cleans files shows a dry run first. Read-only checks never touch the phone.

Outcome

Everything I need to do with a phone now sits one or two keystrokes away: everyday tasks in a couple of taps, safety checks that explain themselves, and safeguards so one careless number cannot break anything. Extended from PhoneSploit Pro by Azeem Idrisi, released under the GNU GPL v3.
